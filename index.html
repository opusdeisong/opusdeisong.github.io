Research Interests

I'm interested in AI agents and value alignment. As LLMs become more capable, they are increasingly embedded in everyday life through tool use and direct interaction with people. With this comes the need for agents that can handle diverse real-world tasks reliably, and for evaluation methods that go beyond surface-level benchmarks to capture what values these systems actually express in practice.

  1. Tool-Use Agents. Enabling language models to use tools effectively so they can explore broader environments and help users solve real-world tasks across diverse scenarios.
  2. Value Alignment. As LLMs take on more societal roles, questions around safety and pluralistic values are becoming central. Yet we still lack adequate instruments to measure what values models hold and how they manifest in context. I work on building better evaluation frameworks for this.

If any of this resonates, I'd love to chat. Reach me at opusdeisong@snu.ac.kr.

ArcANE: Do Role-Playing Language Agents Stay in Character at the Right Time?

EMNLP 2026 (Main)

Woojung Song*, Nalim Kim*, Sangjun Song, Chaewon Heo, Jongwon Lim, Yohan Jo  Co-first

A benchmark of 17 novels and 80 characters testing whether role-playing agents track a character's evolving psychology rather than a fixed persona. Conditioning on a Character Arc—the narrative segmented into psychological phases—beats every other context strategy on all six models, most of all on scenarios beyond the source text.

Human Psychometric Questionnaires Mischaracterize LLM Behavior

EMNLP 2026 (Main)

Woojung Song*, Dongmin Choi*, Yoonah Park, Jongwook Han, Eun-Ju Lee, Yohan Jo  Co-first

LLM profiles from Likert questionnaires (PVQ, BFI) diverge substantially from those observed in generation behavior on realistic user queries: lexical cues in questionnaire items invite socially desirable responses, and persona-induced shifts on questionnaires vanish in actual generation—arguing for generation-based profiling instead.

Don't Adapt Small Language Models for Tools; Adapt Tool Schemas to the Models

ACL 2026 (Main)

Jonggeun Lee*, Woojung Song*, Jongwook Han, Haesung Pyun, Yohan Jo  Co-first

Small models hallucinate tool names shaped by pretraining conventions instead of the given schema. PA-Tool renames schema components toward pretraining-familiar patterns, cutting schema-misalignment errors by 80% and improving tool-use accuracy by up to 17%—without any retraining.

Non-Collaborative User Simulators for Tool Agents

ICLR 2026

Jeonghoon Shim, Woojung Song, Cheyon Jin, Seungwon Kook, Yohan Jo

A user simulator producing four categories of realistic non-collaborative behaviors—unavailable requests, digressions, impatience, and incomplete utterances—that exposes sharp performance drops, escalated hallucinations, and dialogue breakdowns in state-of-the-art tool agents on MultiWOZ and τ-bench.

Value Portrait: Assessing Language Models' Values through Psychometrically and Ecologically Valid Items

ACL 2025 (Main)

Jongwook Han*, Dongmin Choi*, Woojung Song*, Eun-Ju Lee, Yohan Jo  Co-first

A value benchmark built from real user-LLM interactions, psychometrically validated against human raters' own value scores. Across 44 LLMs, models consistently prioritize Benevolence, Security, and Self-Direction over Tradition, Power, and Achievement.
