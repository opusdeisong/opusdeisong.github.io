ArcANE: Do Role-Playing Language Agents Stay in Character at the Right Time?

EMNLP 2026 (Main)

Woojung Song*, Nalim Kim*, Sangjun Song, Chaewon Heo, Jongwon Lim, Yohan Jo  Co-first

An automatically constructed benchmark of 17 novels and 80 principal characters that tests whether role-playing agents track a character's evolving psychology rather than a fixed persona. Conditioning on a Character Arc—the narrative segmented into psychological phases—beats every other context strategy on all six models, with the largest gains on scenarios the source text never explores, and fine-tuned ArcANE-8B/32B widen that gap further.

Human Psychometric Questionnaires Mischaracterize LLM Behavior

EMNLP 2026 (Main)

Woojung Song*, Dongmin Choi*, Yoonah Park, Jongwook Han, Eun-Ju Lee, Yohan Jo  Co-first

Compares the value and personality profiles of eight open LLMs measured two ways: Likert self-reports on established questionnaires (PVQ, BFI) versus generation probabilities on realistic user queries. The two diverge substantially—explicit lexical cues in questionnaire items let models respond in socially desirable ways, and persona-induced shifts on questionnaires vanish in actual generation—showing questionnaires mischaracterize LLM behavior and arguing for generation-based profiling.

Don't Adapt Small Language Models for Tools; Adapt Tool Schemas to the Models

ACL 2026 (Main)

Jonggeun Lee*, Woojung Song*, Jongwook Han, Haesung Pyun, Yohan Jo  Co-first

Small language models fail at tool use largely through schema misalignment: they hallucinate tool names shaped by pretraining conventions instead of the given schema. PA-Tool renames schema components toward pretraining-familiar patterns—selected via a peakedness signal from contamination detection—cutting misalignment errors by 80% and improving accuracy by up to 17% on MetaTool and RoTBench, all without retraining.

Non-Collaborative User Simulators for Tool Agents

ICLR 2026

Jeonghoon Shim, Woojung Song, Cheyon Jin, Seungwon Kook, Yohan Jo

A user simulator producing four categories of realistic non-collaborative behaviors—requesting unavailable services, digressing, expressing impatience, and giving incomplete utterances—while still reliably delivering every intent needed to complete the task. On MultiWOZ and τ-bench, state-of-the-art tool agents degrade sharply against these users, with escalated hallucinations and dialogue breakdowns.

Psychometric Item Validation Using Virtual Respondents with Trait-Response Mediators

TACL, 2026

Sungjib Lim, Woojung Song, Eun-Ju Lee, Yohan Jo

Validates survey items by simulating LLM virtual respondents that account for mediators—the factors through which the same trait can produce different answers to an item—and keeping only items whose responses stay robustly correlated with the intended trait across mediators. Experiments on Big Five, Schwartz values, and VIA character strengths show the framework reliably identifies high-validity items without costly human data collection.

Quantifying Data Contamination in Psychometric Evaluations of LLMs

EACL 2026 (Findings)

Jongwook Han*, Woojung Song*, Jonggeun Lee*, Yohan Jo  Co-first

The first systematic framework for quantifying data contamination in psychometric evaluations, measuring three aspects: item memorization, evaluation memorization, and target score matching. Applied to 21 models and four widely used inventories, it shows that popular instruments like BFI-44 and PVQ-40 are strongly contaminated—models not only memorize items but can steer their responses to hit specific target scores.

Value Portrait: Assessing Language Models' Values through Psychometrically and Ecologically Valid Items

ACL 2025 (Main)

Jongwook Han*, Dongmin Choi*, Woojung Song*, Eun-Ju Lee, Yohan Jo  Co-first

A value benchmark built from real user-LLM interactions, where each item is psychometrically validated against human raters' own value scores rather than annotator intuition. Evaluating 44 LLMs reveals they consistently prioritize Benevolence, Security, and Self-Direction over Tradition, Power, and Achievement—and perceive demographic groups in ways that deviate from real human data.

PeerJ CS, 2026

Jaehyuk Lee*, Woojung Song*, Jina Kim, Yoona Chung, Eunchan Kim  Co-first

Predicts private-brand purchases from real transaction data on a pet e-commerce platform with segment-specific XGBoost models (F1 ≈ 0.78) interpreted via SHAP, finding that dog owners respond more strongly to delivery convenience while cat owners show greater price sensitivity.
